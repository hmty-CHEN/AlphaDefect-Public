# The Silent line (2026-11-09 → 11-12)

When the Regent line stopped on 2026-11-09 (Regent DR #29), its rented RTX 4090 machine started training **the Silent** (静默猎手; the records call it 猎人, "Hunter") from scratch. It used the same network and training system, Muon on the hidden weight matrices from the first update, and a tuning sequence copied from the Defect and Regent lines. Training stopped on 2026-11-11 at 15:00 CET at v24,721, about 49.5 hours after the start. The final network, the EMA copy of v24,721, clears 66.2% of 1,000 fresh W1 seeds greedily at A10 and 69.5% when the two Act-3 bosses are played with search (Silent DR #15). Training resumed that evening from this network, with gradient accumulation ×2 (Silent DR #17). On 2026-11-12 the EMA copy of v26,428 cleared 71.2% of the same 1,000 seeds greedily and 74.0% with Act-3 search, 5.0 and 4.5 pp more than v24,721 on paired seeds (p = 0.004 and 0.006; Silent DR #22, #23). Training continues.

## 1. Starting point

**What carried over.** The handover had planned a separate machine; in the end the Silent took over the Regent's 4090. Nearly everything else came from the Regent line (Silent DR #1). The network is the same: d = 256, 6 layers, 4 heads, FFN ×4, 19 zones, ~19.4M parameters ([04](04_observation_and_model.md)). So are the training system and the rewards ([05](05_training_rewards_curriculum.md)): +0.02 per floor, +0.5 for each of the Act-1 and Act-2 bosses, +1.5 and +2.0 for the two Act-3 bosses, +2.0 for a clear. The engine's truncation rules (Regent DR #10, #13, #20) and the practice shares from Regent DR #11 also carried over, with half the engines playing full runs. So did the rules for later moves: curriculum shifts (Regent DR #15, #18, #19, #22, after Defect DR #82, #105, #120), the plateau rule, and the learning-rate step (Regent DR #21, after Defect DR #222). The Silent thus skipped the progress-gated curriculum that had stalled the Regent for its first 2,685 versions.

**The Silent and observation format 21.** The Silent starts with 70 HP, a 12-card deck (5 Strike, 5 Defend, Neutralize, Survivor) and Ring of the Snake, which draws 2 extra cards at the start of each fight. Its card pool revolves around three mechanics. **Poison** is a stack that deals its value at the start of the enemy's turn, ignores block, then drops by one; Accelerant makes it resolve more than once. **Shivs** are 0-cost exhausting attacks generated by other cards. **Discard** synergies include Sly cards, which play themselves when discarded during your turn, and relics and cards that pay per discard.

Format 21 is the Regent's format 20 with four additions: next-turn poison damage per enemy with a "poison kills it" flag; damage previews for Knife Trap and Outbreak; on-play riders (Strangle, Serpent Form, Afterimage); and counters for cards discarded this turn and drawn this fight. Checked against the engine's true state, poison damage matched actual HP loss 720/720 times, and every flagged enemy died (806/806). Defect and Regent frames stayed byte-identical (Silent DR #1; `zh/观测格式.md` §四之三).

**Muon from the first update.** The Regent's mid-training Muon trial (Regent DR #25) made training worse within 750 versions. At a KL-calibrated muon_lr of 5.5e-4, each version moved the weights about four times as far as Adam did, and on a plateau dominated by noise that movement is extra noise. The trial was rolled back (Regent DR #27). Silent DR #1 argued that training from scratch is dominated by signal, which is where Muon should help.

On the Silent, Muon (momentum 0.95, Nesterov, 5 Newton–Schulz steps; code from Regent DR #25) updates the 44 hidden 2-D weight matrices: 8,425,472 parameters, ~43% of the total. Adam updates the embeddings, the small input projections, the output layers, the biases and LayerNorm. The starting muon_lr was 6e-4, about twice the Adam rate and between the equal-RMS and equal-KL conversions measured on the Regent. The other starting settings: Adam 3e-4, 32,768 steps per version, minibatch 4,096, 2 epochs, target_kl 0.03, EMA 0.999. At v458 the KL median was 0.0122 and the ratio clipping 0.120, inside the Regent's healthy band, so muon_lr stayed. At v1,060 the Adam part went to 4e-4, as the handover had planned; no read of that change was recorded (Silent DR #2).

Whether Muon helped is **not shown**. At v1,269 (41.6M steps; all three lines used 32,768 steps per version at that stage), training full runs reached floor 20.6 for the Silent, 16.2 for the Regent and 12.3 for the Defect. In evaluation, 79% of runs reached Act 2, against 48% and 35%. The log warns that the different character and the Regent's stalled curriculum explain part of the gap. There is no Adam control.

**Evaluation and checkpoints.** Routine evaluation plays the **raw** learner weights greedily on 384 fixed seeds from pool P1 per round. Silent DR #4, made at the owner's request for denser evaluation, moved inference from CPU to GPU: a round now takes 218 s instead of 430–510 s, for 1.8% less training throughput (measured over ~45 versions). The Regent had no uniformly spaced checkpoints before v15,520 (Regent DR #28), so the Silent keeps them from the start. It saves a learner checkpoint every 20 versions, with optimizer state, EMA and Muon momentum, and a backup every 30 minutes, hard-linked into a folder that is never rotated. A tagged backup precedes every configuration change. Every per-run evaluation result and full-run replay is kept, and each run records the commit id of the deployed code.

## 3. The changes and their evidence

**Evidence levels** are adapted from [02](02_timeline_and_critical_path.md). *Attributed (before/after)*: the change was alone in its window, and the readings moved well beyond the pre-change trend. No Silent change had a paired control, so this is weaker than "attributed" in [02]. *Confounded*: the readings moved, but general growth or another change overlapped. *Not shown*: no measurable effect, or never read.

**How readings are taken.** Evaluation compares the last 12 rounds (4,608 runs) with the 10 or 12 rounds before the change; one round's standard error is ~2.5 pp at a ~45% clear rate. Training full runs, which use sampled play and clear less often than greedy play, and practice win rates are compared over same-length windows of 250–1,000 versions. Practice positions come from the agent's own runs, so their difficulty drifts. After #8 and #11, one version holds 2× and then 4× the original data.

### 3.1 Act-1 boss milestone (#3)

The Silent beat the Act-1 boss early. From ~20M steps, only 16–19% of evaluation runs died there, against 68% for the Regent at the same point, though at equal overall level the two were similar (88.1% vs 87.7% per fight). After asking whether the first boss was obviously easy for the Silent, the owner cut the reward for entering Act 2 from 0.5 to 0.1. The owner supervised this change personally, so no guardrail was set, and no read was recorded. **Not shown.**

The inherited rule: when a practice category saturates (Act-2 normal fights at 96% over 500 versions; Act-2 elites at 92%, later 97%), part of its share goes to the boss that kills most often, one change at a time, with a check after 750 versions.

#5 and #6 landed during fast general growth: clears rose from 3.41% before #6 to 28.4% before #7, about 3 pp per 1,000 versions, so neither change can be separated from that growth. #6 split its freed 0.10 by deaths, because the Act-3 bosses killed 2.9× as many runs as the Act-2 boss; the Regent had split evenly. #7 was checked against a trend fitted to 42 rounds (+2.61 pp per 1,000 versions, residual SD 2.8), and its 11 rounds read −1.24 ± 0.84 pp against that trend. The 500-version evaluation blocks read 49.8, 51.0 and 51.5% before the change and 54.07, 55.21 and 55.45% after. Per fight, the Act-3 bosses went 89.9% → 92.0% and 72.6% → 77.8%, and Test Subject 73.3% → 79.1%. The same move on the Regent showed no extra gain (Regent DR #22). ### 3.3 Halving the learning rate and doubling the batch, twice (#8, #11)

**#8 (v18,638).** At v18,610 the plateau rule fired. Practice on the Act-2 and Act-3 bosses had moved −1.1 and −0.1 pp between the last two 1,000-version windows. Training full-run clears had sat at 17.5–20.1% since v16,750, and the evaluation slope had fallen from +2.61 to +1.10 pp per 1,000 versions. Following Regent DR #21, the agent halved both learning rates (Adam 2e-4, Muon 3e-4) and doubled the batch to 65,536 steps, or 32 gradient steps per version. Halving Muon had no precedent. It was needed because Muon's step size depends only on muon_lr: halving only the Adam part would have left 43% of the parameters at the old step size. About 1,260 versions later, training full runs read 34.44% over the last 500 versions (20,768 runs), against ~18.2% before. Evaluation read 46.88% against 29.17% (12 rounds), and Act-3 boss practice 47.6% against 31.7–35.0%. No harm from halving Muon was visible.

**#11 (v23,321).** By 2026-11-11, evaluation had been flat for ~1,700 versions (500-version blocks 54.07, 55.21, 55.45 and 53.43%), and training clears sat at 40.3–41.8%. The agent's plan was gradient accumulation ×2 first (Silent DR #10 addendum). The owner asked instead to repeat #8: Adam 1e-4, Muon 1.5e-4, 131,072 steps per version. By the plan's own measure (learning rate ÷ √minibatch), this put the noise temperature at about 0.25× its starting value. That is below the ~0.35× at which the Defect last gained (Defect DR #225). At 500 versions, evaluation read 62.50% against 53.43% (12 rounds), and the jump came within the first 250 versions (blocks of 54.7, 56.3, 53.1 and 54.1% before, 60.4 and 62.5% after). The Act-3 bosses went 91.1% → 93.1% and 76.0% → 81.2%, and training full runs 41.01% → 47.18%.

Both changes are **attributed (before/after) as packages**. Each was alone in its window; format 23 was held back until #8 had been read. Each followed a flat stretch, and evaluation moved by 17.7 and 9.1 pp, far more than the ~3 pp drift between independent timelines (§3.5). The learning-rate cut and the batch increase were never tested separately. The log explains both results through Regent DR #25–#27: on a plateau dominated by noise, a smaller step settles closer to a good point.

### 3.4 Observation format 23 (#9)

Around v17,853, two Act-3 bosses were the main killers: Test Subject ended 15.9% of runs and Aeonglass 12.1%. The policy played poison, block and draw, mostly with skills. Its most-picked cards per run were Backflip (1.04), Deflect (0.88) and Snakebite (0.72), and it rarely took shiv cards. A second observation review found that candidate feature 10, "HP lost if I play this and end the turn", ignored three things this deck does all the time. It ignored Weak and Strength-down applied this turn (Neutralize, Leg Sweep, Malaise, potions), which already reduce this turn's enemy attack. It ignored enemy growth triggered by the play: Test Subject's Enrage adds Strength for every skill played in its first phase, and Infested Prism's Vital Spark adds damage when infected cards are played. And it ignored enemies that the play makes poison-killable before they act.

Format 23 adds these, plus format 22's thorns and Aeonglass's Withering Presence. Only feature values change: the layout is the same, no vocabulary was added and the network was not modified. Self-consistency for Weak and Strength-down plays rose from 920/1,326 to 1,325/1,326. On 60 replayed evaluation runs, 6.9% of the model's actual plays saw a changed value, and in Test Subject fights 16% did.

About 1,385 versions later, training full runs read 38.26% against 34.44%, with the ~3 pp step coming in the first 250 versions, and evaluation read 50.65% against 46.88%. But **the targeted fights did not improve**. Test Subject went from 74.5% to 73.3% per fight, Kaiser Crab stayed at 92.6%, and deaths to Infested Prism went from 1.02% to 1.04% of runs. **Confounded** with the tail of #8, and kept because it did no harm.

### 3.5 Longer run segments, withdrawn (#12, #13)

Combat advantages run to the end of each fight. Out-of-combat advantages run along the chain of out-of-combat decisions and are bootstrapped at the end of each segment. With 64-step segments, about 70% of the λ-return weight per decision fell on the bootstrapped value; with 256 steps, it would be about 40%. The agent proposed 256, and the owner approved it.

At 500 versions, training full runs read 45.66% against 47.19% (~39,200 runs each). Out-of-combat entropy had risen from 0.302 to 0.343 and was still climbing, and out-of-combat value loss had gone from 0.238 to 0.422. Evaluation read 61.28% over the last 10 rounds, against 62.00%. The guardrails, set at baseline −2 pp, never fired. The read rule "out-of-combat value clearly worse and clears not up" withdrew the change. The inferred mechanism is that out-of-combat advantages are normalized per batch, so extra return noise shrinks the normalized signal while the fixed entropy bonus gains relative weight.

Silent DR #13 put segments back to 64 and, at the owner's request, restored the v23,827 checkpoint, discarding 517 versions. The logs keep them, but the dashboard and the scripts drop superseded version numbers. A same-start comparison over 193 versions favoured 64 on sampled play: training full runs read 47.86% vs 45.83% (~15,000 runs each, SE ~0.6), and Act-3 boss practice 0.615 vs 0.602. Greedy evaluation pointed the other way: 59.5% in the first 9 rounds after the revert, against 62.6% in the first 10 rounds of #12, and the restored checkpoint's own round read 57.3%. **The log corrected its earlier reading.** The evaluation dip during #12 cannot be blamed on 256, because greedy evaluation of raw weights drifts by ~3 pp between independent timelines over 200–300 versions. That is more than 12-round averages can resolve. The withdrawal rests on sampled play alone. At the close (v24,207), training full runs read 47.95% (29,302 runs) and evaluation 61.96%.

### 3.6 Skipping fights the model is sure to win (#14)

Silent DR #14, live from v24,208, grew out of combat-outcome tests for which training paused twice on 2026-11-11 (for 16 and 47 minutes). In training full runs, a normal or elite fight entered from the map is skipped with probability 0.8 when the auxiliary head's win probability on its first frame is at least 0.995 and the encounter is not blacklisted. Outcome heads then sample the HP and potion use after the fight, and a new engine request ends the fight through the game's normal victory flow. Boss fights, practice and routine evaluation stay real, and 25% of full runs are played fully real as a control. In 1,038 real runs, such fights made up 82.9% of all fights and were lost only 8 times; outside boss fights they took ~55% of run steps. The aim is more full runs per unit of compute, not a better signal.

Any of four pre-registered guardrails turns skipping off: evaluation below 59.5% over 12 rounds; HP entering the Act-3 bosses differing by more than 3 pp between skip-allowed and fully real runs; potion predictions off by more than 0.05; or stuck runs and engine errors above 0.1%. The read at 500 versions (v24,208–v24,707, against v23,708–v24,207) found the throughput the design aimed for and no measurable change in strength. Full runs per hour went from 13,741 to 18,310 (+33%) and out-of-combat steps per version from 10,803 to 15,295 (+42%), while each run still made ~140 out-of-combat decisions. Fully real runs cleared 48.48% ± 0.43 (13,747 runs) against 47.67% ± 0.25 before (38,512 runs, all real); per 100 versions they read 49.3, 49.0, 48.8, 48.5 and 46.9%, so the rise before launch had already reached ~48% and did not continue. Routine evaluation read 62.60% over the 28 rounds since launch (62.43% over the last 12) against 60.96% over the 20 rounds of #13, within the ~3 pp drift of §3.5. No guardrail came near firing: HP entering the Act-3 bosses was 0.900 in fully real and 0.899 in skip-allowed runs, potion use 0.215 predicted vs 0.211 actual, and no run got stuck in 56,326. Out-of-combat entropy stayed at 0.305 → 0.307; the combat-step entropy rose from 0.672 to 0.694 only because the fights still played are the harder ones. **Throughput gained; no effect on strength shown.**

The same read looked at evaluation noise. Over those 28 rounds, 348 of the 384 fixed seeds changed outcome at least once, and adjacent rounds differed on ~28% of seeds. The per-round SD (1.8 pp) is close to the 1.96 pp expected if each seed flips independently at its own win rate. The log concludes that the noise comes from the weights moving between versions, and that more seeds per round would not remove it. The full story is in [21](21_combat_outcome_prediction_and_skipping.md).

### 3.7 Closing tests: snapshot ensembles, EMA weights and search (#15)

Shortly before 15:00 on 2026-11-11 the owner asked whether there were enough checkpoints to run the Regent's closing sequence: snapshot ensemble, distillation, search evaluation, release. After the #14 read, training and routine evaluation stopped at v24,721 (15:00 CET). Eight training batches (v24,599–v24,680, ~1.05M steps) had been saved beforehand for distillation.

**How far apart the snapshots are.** This was measured as in the Regent DR #24 addendum: the share of 4,096 decision states where two networks' top choices differ. Against v24,460, a snapshot 240 versions earlier differs on 20.1%, one 1,100 versions earlier on 26.3%, and the curve flattens at ~30% after ~2,000 versions. On the Regent, snapshots 260 versions apart differed on only 12.8% (plateau 19–21%); the Silent learns from twice as much data per version and was still improving. The seven snapshots v23,360–v24,460 differ pairwise by 21.0% on average, more than the Regent's E8 (19.0%). The EMA copy of v24,460 differs from its own raw weights on 20.5% but from the seven-snapshot ensemble on only 10.6%: the EMA already averages out most of the snapshot-to-snapshot jitter. Only snapshots after #11 (evaluation 60–63%) were used.

**Ensemble test** (the first 1,000 seeds of pool W1, never used for the Silent; greedy, A10, action probabilities averaged per step):

| Arm | Network | Clear | Mean floor | Reach Act 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M | EMA weights of v24,721 | 662 / 1,000 = 66.2% | 44.64 | 86.0% |
| E | ensemble of the raw weights of v24,721, 24,460, 24,220, 24,043, 23,880, 23,720, 23,540, 23,360 | 656 / 1,000 = 65.6% | 45.00 | 87.1% |
| S | raw weights of v24,721 | 656 / 1,000 = 65.6% | 44.41 | 85.0% |

Paired on the same seeds: M vs S +0.6 ± 3.5 pp (160 vs 154 seeds won by only one arm, p = 0.78); E vs S +0.0 (155 vs 155); M vs E +0.6 ± 3.2 (p = 0.76). Each pair of arms disagreed on 266–314 seeds, so they do play differently, but the wins and losses cancel. This is the opposite of the Regent (E8 +5.2 pp, EMA +4.4 pp) and matches the Defect (EMA 73.6% vs raw 74.0%, Defect DR #265). The snapshots differ more than the Regent's, so "the snapshots are too close" does not explain it; on the Silent the ensemble averages away differences that do not decide wins. With a teacher no stronger than the student's starting point, **distillation was skipped**. The final network is arm M, the EMA weights of v24,721: a single network, no worse than the raw weights in the paired test, with smoother weights. (The single network's 65.6% on W1 is higher than the 62.4% of the last 12 P1 rounds because the seed sets differ.)

**Act-3 search.** The two Act-3 bosses were played with PIMC search in sts2-sim, as on the Regent and the Defect (the Silent adaptation of sts2-sim was uncommitted work and is not in this repository), on the same 1,000 seeds: **66.2% → 69.5%** (+3.3 ± 1.5 pp, McNemar exact p = 3.8 × 10⁻⁵; 95% CI of the 69.5% about 66.6–72.3%). The greedy arm of this tool reproduced arm M run for run. Of the 839 runs that reached the first Act-3 boss, search won both bosses in 695 and greedy play in 662; 48 runs were won only with search and 15 only without. Search ran on 69,163 decisions, changed the greedy choice on 10.6% of them and fell back to greedy on 0.8%, at 0.71 s per search on average, with no errors in ~1.94 million simulated worlds. In runs both arms won, search ended with 3.9 pp more HP on average. The same search added +3.1 pp on the Regent (Regent DR #29) and +3.6 to +4.9 pp on the Defect (DR #290).

### 3.8 Training resumed from the EMA weights (#16, #17)

During the stop, the machine played 9,000 more games with the final network (8,000 greedy and a second batch of 1,000 with Act-3 search) to help train a small win-probability model that is used outside training and is not released (Silent DR #16). The owner then asked to resume training "from the distillation point". Nothing had been distilled, so the agent took the released network. In the learner checkpoint, the raw weights were replaced by the checkpoint's own EMA copy (174 of 175 entries; the remaining one is an integer buffer), and Adam and Muon state, the outcome heads and the EMA itself were kept (Silent DR #17). The only configuration change was gradient accumulation 1 → 2 (effective minibatch 8,192, learning rates unchanged). This was the next step of the owner's plateau plan of 2026-11-10, chosen because the #14 read had shown a flat curve. KL per version fell from 0.0053 to 0.0026–0.0031, about half, as expected from half as many optimizer steps.

The read at 500 versions (v24,722–v25,221, against #14's v24,208–v24,707) was clearly positive. Fully real training runs cleared 53.07% (13,227 runs) against 48.48%; routine evaluation read 65.33% over 29 rounds against 62.85% (64.34% over the last 12); boss practice for the three acts went 0.687 / 0.666 / 0.620 → 0.697 / 0.674 / 0.654; KL fell from 0.0051 to 0.0031. The gain came at once, with the new start point, and did not continue: per 100 versions, fully real runs stayed between 52.3% and 54.3%. **Two changes at once**, so the new start point and the accumulation cannot be separated; the timing points to the start point. Training was paused once in this stretch, for 89 minutes at v24,960, for the diagnostic of §3.10.

### 3.9 An observation fix kept on a branch (#18)

The analysis of Act-3 boss losses after #15 found four small gaps in format 23: end-of-turn passive damage ignored Intangible, Beating Remnant's per-turn damage cap was missing from the end-turn preview, the Queen's minions dying with her was not modelled, and defensive potions did not reach candidate features 8–10. ### 3.10 Is the policy locked out of long card plans? (#19)

The owner asked whether training can lock a policy into strategies that pay off after few decisions, because those are learned first. On the Silent the obvious candidates are shivs and Sly cards: they pay off only after several more picks, and the policy almost never takes them (below 2% of offers). Silent DR #19 tested this with a frozen plan and frozen decision rules. The engine's branch reset re-rolls the rest of a run from any decision with common random numbers, so the same state can be played on with different choices.

At 1,500–2,850 card-reward screens per class, where the greedy policy had skipped the class, arm A kept the policy's choice and arm B forced the class's most likely card. Arm C also forced it and added a logit bias β (set so that about half of later offers of the class would be taken) at every later card-reward screen. Three classes were tested: shiv generators and payoffs (S), Sly payoffs (Y) and, as a control, single-card attacks that work on their own (K). An "ability ladder" repeated A and B with the raw weights of v20,320 and v22,320 (routine evaluation about 49.5% and 55.8%). If a class's value depended on later skill, forcing it should hurt less for stronger networks than for the control.

| Class | B − A, final network | B − A at v20,320 / v22,320 | Ladder interaction minus control | C − B |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| K (control) | −10.3 ± 1.8 pp | −10.1 / −10.9 | — | — |
| S (shivs) | −8.7 ± 1.7 | −10.9 / −7.9 | +2.4 ± 3.5 | −5.9 ± 1.7 |
| Y (Sly payoffs) | −9.5 ± 2.4 | −8.5 / −7.3 | −0.8 ± 4.2 | −4.9 ± 1.8 |

Forcing a pick cost 9–10 pp of clear rate for every class, the control included. Most of that cost is losing the card the policy wanted: where the original choice was to skip, forcing cost only −3.2 (K), −7.6 (S) and −2.8 pp (Y). The interaction with skill is near zero, and steering toward the class made things worse (C − B). Under the frozen rules both classes are **"weak for this policy"**, and there is **no evidence of a lock-in**. The test bounds the skill dependence of shivs relative to the control at about 6 pp, and of Sly payoffs at about 3.4 pp, over the range from ~50% to ~66% clear rate. It does not show what targeted practice with shivs would achieve. A planned Act-3 search arm could not run: sts2-sim cannot import a run whose Act-3 map the branch reset has regenerated. Replay checks matched 240/240, and no run got stuck.

### 3.11 Skipping every eligible fight, withdrawn (#20, #21)

The owner asked whether full runs could stop training on fights, or train only on their boss fights. The agent advised against both: the Defect's stronger version of this (Defect DR #267) had shown no gain, and fights inside full runs are the only data that teach HP trade-offs against the real outcome of a run. It proposed instead to raise #14's skip probability from 0.8 to 1.0, and the owner agreed, to take effect after the #17 read. Fully real runs (25%) were kept, so in skip-allowed runs every eligible fight was skipped. This went live at v25,228 (Silent DR #20). The share of eligible fights skipped went from 0.60 to 0.75, and full runs per version from 105 to 118.

At 250 versions the training guardrail fired:

|---|---|---|---|

The loss was concentrated in the Act-3 bosses. Act-3 boss practice fell from the first version and was still falling at 250 (0.632 over the last 100). Practice uses saved real positions and HP entering the bosses did not change, so the positions did not get harder; the policy got worse at these fights. ## 4. What moved the number

1. **The two learning-rate and batch packages** (#8, #11): +17.7 and +9.1 pp in evaluation; attributed (before/after) as packages.
2. **Shifting practice to the Act-3 bosses**: #10 is probably positive; #5 and #6 cannot be separated from growth; #7 had no detectable effect.
3. **Format 23**: confounded; the fights it targeted did not improve.
4. **Muon from scratch**: a fast start, but no control. As a rough pace only, the Silent's evaluation averaged 13.9% over v11,000–11,999 and 19.8% over v14,000–14,999. The Regent's 10-round average reached 10% at v24,659 and 20% at v27,590, and the Defect's at v45,625 and v59,288 (handover; all at ~33k steps per version). Characters, curricula and averaging differ.
5. **Act-1 reward cut** (#3): unknown. **256-step segments** (#12): negative, withdrawn. **Skipping easy fights** (#14): 1.33× full runs per hour, no strength gain shown over 500 versions.
6. **At the first stop** (#15): neither the EMA weights nor an 8-snapshot ensemble beat the raw weights (+0.6 and +0.0 pp on 1,000 paired seeds); Act-3 search added 3.3 pp.
7. **Restart from the EMA weights with accumulation ×2** (#17): fully real runs 48.5% → 53.1% and evaluation 62.9% → 65.3% at once; the two parts were not separated. By v26,428 the EMA weights were 5.0 pp above the first release on paired seeds (#22).
8. **Skipping every eligible fight** (#20): Act-3 boss practice and fully real runs fell; withdrawn after 257 versions (#21), and both recovered.
9. **No card-choice lock-in found** (#19): forcing shiv or Sly cards cost as much as forcing control cards, and the cost did not shrink for stronger networks.

## 5. How the line is supervised

The Silent has its own agent session. The owner sets direction and has delegated in stages: first practice shares and fixes for abnormal hyperparameters, to be recorded afterwards (Silent DR #5), then "take over training for a while" (Silent DR #10, #12). The owner initiated #3, #4, #9, #11, #14 and the weight revert in #13. The agent initiated #5–#8 and #10 under delegation (the owner confirmed #7) and proposed #12, which the owner approved. After the restart, the owner asked for #16, #17, #19, #22 and #23 and approved #20, which the agent had proposed. While the owner was away, the agent made #21 alone, under the standing instruction to decide without asking.

A supervision script polls the learner and evaluation logs every 5 minutes. It wakes the session on an ALERT (learner log silent for 10 minutes, non-finite gradients, a KL median above 0.04, entropy out of bounds, a slow evaluation round) or a TRIGGER (a pre-agreed decision point such as "Act-2 normal practice at 96%"). Since 2026-11-10, it also reports at least once an hour. Nothing rolls back automatically.

Every change is **pre-registered** in its log entry before launch. The entry sets the baseline window and the guardrails, usually the baseline minus 2–3 pp on evaluation and training clears, armed after 8 evaluation rounds or 500 versions. It also sets a read point (500 or 750 versions) and the rollback: a tagged backup plus the previous configuration. After each restart, the session checks the configuration checksums, the new values in the restart log, that Muon momentum and EMA carried over from the checkpoint, and the measured practice shares. The #12–#13 episode taught two lessons. Guardrails at baseline −2 pp never fired on a slow decline; the scheduled read caught it. And after the weight revert, the evaluation guardrail's start version was not updated, so it stayed inactive until an hourly check noticed. #20–#21 showed the opposite case: one pre-registered line fired, the rule needed two, and the agent rolled back on the shape of the decline (concentrated in one fight type, still falling, cheap to undo). The recovery confirmed that call.

The diagnostic (#19) and the closing tests (#15, #22, #23) paused training. Each pause stops the learner and routine evaluation, keeps a tagged backup hard-linked into the permanent folder, and on restart checks that optimizer state, Muon momentum, EMA and outcome heads were carried over.

## 7. Open questions

1. **Does skipping pay off, and why did skipping everything hurt?** At 0.8, skipping gave 1.33× full runs per hour and no measurable gain over 500 versions, at a point where training was already flat. At 1.0 it cost the Act-3 bosses about 2 pp (#20). Why skipping the last easy fights should weaken boss play is unexplained.
2. **When do EMA weights help?** At v24,721 the EMA weights tied with the raw weights (#15), as on the Defect; at v26,428 they were 4.8 pp better (#22), as on the Regent. Accumulation ×2 and the EMA start point are the candidate causes; neither was tested. Ensembles have never beaten the EMA single network on the Silent.
3. **Evaluation resolution.** Timelines drift by ~3 pp, so changes of 1–2 pp need paired tests on fresh seeds ([06](06_evaluation_methodology.md)).
4. **What is left of noise reduction?** The noise temperature is already ~0.25× its starting value, below where the Defect stopped gaining, yet #11 worked, and #17 (accumulation ×2) coincided with another gain. The planned next step on a plateau is halving both learning rates.
5. **Act 1.** The Act-1 boss is the second-largest single cause of death (5.97% of runs before #11). Whether the #3 reward cut contributes has not been checked.
6. **Muon versus Adam from scratch**, and halving Muon along with Adam, have never been controlled.
7. **Card plans that take several picks** (shivs, Sly payoffs): no lock-in found (#19), but whether targeted practice would make them good for this policy is untested.
8. **Longer out-of-combat horizons** failed under per-batch normalization and a fixed entropy bonus. The log proposes a "to the end of the act" truncation with per-act baselines as the next try.
