Scope note: research done 2026-10-31 via web search/fetch. Some PDFs could not be parsed (LoCM SBGames 2020, U. Alberta CoG 2024 MTG drafting) and are cited by abstract/snippet only. Items marked "(not fetched this session)" are cited from well-known primary sources whose content I did not re-read today; the report writer should treat them as lower-confidence. Formulas and power numbers that I derived myself are placed under "Inferences", not "Cited Findings".

Project numbers used below (from the assignment, not verified): 1000 fixed seeds, greedy policy, win rate p ≈ 0.60, 95% CI ≈ ±3 points, paired sign tests on discordant seeds, value head with r = 0.92 against MC rollouts on boss states, StS2 v0.111.0, Defect A10.

## 1. Which variance-reduction estimators exist (AIVAT/DIVAT/MIVAT, luck adjustment, control variates, IS/DR, CRN/antithetics), how much do they reduce variance, and what are their preconditions?

### Takeaway
Value-function control variates that subtract "luck" at chance events (MIVAT) and, where strategies are known, at the agents' own randomised actions (AIVAT) are provably unbiased for *any* fixed value function. Published reductions run from about 25% to over 99% in standard deviation. Standard deviation falling to a third (roughly a 10x saving in games) is the commonly quoted practical case. Backgammon bots have used the same idea ("luck-adjusted results") since Jellyfish and report 3x to 10x+ fewer trials. Importance-weighted and doubly robust estimators need a stochastic behaviour policy, so they do not suit a greedy deterministic agent.

### Cited Findings
**AIVAT (Burch, Schmid, Moravčík, Morrill, Bowling; AAAI 2018, arXiv 1612.06915)**
- Described as a "low-variance, provably unbiased" evaluator that uses a heuristic value estimate plus the known strategy of some agents. It reduces variance from both chance events and player decisions, while MIVAT covers chance only. — [AAAI page](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/11481)
- Estimator form: AIVAT(z) = [Σ_{z'∈W} π_Pa(z') v_p(z') / Σ_{z'∈W} π_Pa(z')] + Σ_{H} k_H(z). Each correction term is k_H(z) = [Σ_a Σ_{h∈H} π_Pa(h·a) u_h(a) / Σ_{h∈H} π_Pa(h)] − [Σ_{h∈H} π_Pa(h·a_O) u_h(a_O) / Σ_{h∈H} π_Pa(h·a_O)], where a_O is the observed action. In words, each term is the expected value over the known-strategy or chance distribution minus the value of what actually happened. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1612.06915)
- Preconditions: P_a, the set of players whose strategies are known, must include chance. If P_a = {chance}, AIVAT reduces to MIVAT. The u_h(a) "can be arbitrary fixed functions". Unbiasedness (Lemma 1, Theorem 1) holds whatever the value function's quality, and quality affects only how much variance is removed. Partition conditions: states in an information partition must have identical opponent reach probabilities, and no state in a partition may precede another. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1612.06915)
- Leduc hold'em self-play, 100k games: SD 3.513 for raw chips, 2.327 for MIVAT (−33.8%), 1.928 for MIVAT+imaginary observations (−45.1%), 0.00643 for AIVAT with one known strategy (−99.8%), and 0.00377 with both strategies known (≈−99.9%). — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1612.06915)
- Leduc, dissimilar strategies: SD 5.761 for chips, 4.412 for MIVAT (~−23%), 4.295 for MIVAT+IO (−25%), and 1.437 to 2.983 for AIVAT (−48% to −75%, depending on which strategies are known). — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1612.06915)
- Heads-up no-limit hold'em, 1M games: SD 25.96 for chips, 21.29 for MIVAT, 16.07 for MIVAT+IO (−39%), and 8.10 for AIVAT (a bit more than −68%). The authors say a roughly 3x SD reduction reaches the same significance with "ten times less data". — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1612.06915)
- DeepStack's match evaluation used AIVAT, with its own value estimates as the control variate. This cut the standard deviation by about 85%. The win-rate estimate of ~486 mbb/g was more than 20 SD from zero. — [DeepStack, arXiv 1701.01724](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.01724)
- A later survey reports AIVAT with lower variance than MIVAT and imaginary observations, with a 68% SD drop on self-play data. — [Search in Imperfect Information Games, arXiv 2111.05884](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.05884)
- AIVAT produced significant results "with as few as 28 matches" in a short AI-vs-human series. — [mlanthology entry](https://mlanthology.org/aaai/2018/burch2018aaai-aivat)

**DIVAT / MIVAT lineage (U. Alberta)**
- DIVAT uses value functions as a control variate for the expected utility given observed chance events, and requires a strategy for all players so it can generate self-play value estimates. MIVAT generalises DIVAT to arbitrary value functions defined after chance events, adding a correction per chance event. MIVAT does not account for "lucky" actions drawn from a randomised strategy, and AIVAT was built to fix that. — [AIVAT arXiv PDF](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1612.06915); [Bowling group, "Optimal Unbiased Estimators for Evaluating Agent Performance", AAAI 2006](https://webdocs.cs.ualberta.ca/~bowling/publications/b2hd-06aaai-divat.html) (author list not verified in this session)
- Davidson's MSc thesis on "baseline" estimators reports them as competitive with state-of-the-art efficient-evaluation techniques in computer poker variants. — [Davidson MSc thesis](https://www.cs.ualberta.ca/~games/poker/publications/jdavidson.msc.pdf) (snippet only)

**Backgammon luck adjustment (closest non-poker analogue: single-agent-like dice luck)**
- GNU Backgammon applies variance reduction automatically when it looks ahead at least one ply, using the equity difference from one ply to the next. — [GNUBG manual](https://www.gnu.org/software/gnubg/manual/html_node/Rollouts-in-GNU-Backgammon.html)
- "Quasi-random dice" stratify the first roll so that every block of 36 trials covers each opening roll evenly, and GNUBG also rotates and stratifies at multiples of 36 and 1296. — [GNUBG manual, Quasi-Random Dice](https://www.gnu.org/software/gnubg/manual/html_node/Quasi_002dRandom-Dice.html)
- Douglas Zare: if the bot understands the positions in a rollout, variance reduction can cut the trials needed "by a factor of 10 or more". If it does not understand them well, the factor may be "only 3". — [bkgm.com, Zare on rollouts](https://bkgm.com/articles/Zare/Rollouts/)
- Variance reduction "doesn't guarantee the rollouts will be correct". It only reduces the trials needed. GNUBG's luck-adjusted match results were modelled on rollout variance reduction and are much faster to compute than many rollouts. The idea traces to David Montgomery's description of Jellyfish. — [bug-gnubg 2003](https://mail.gnu.org/archive/html/bug-gnubg/2003-06/msg00182.html); [bug-gnubg 2006](https://lists.libreplanet.org/archive/html/bug-gnubg/2006-07/msg00026.html)
- Caveat raised by users: the luck estimate depends on GNUBG's own evaluator, so a misjudged position "shows up as luck". This affects the variance and the per-game luck/skill attribution, not unbiasedness of the mean. — [bug-gnubg 2003](https://mail.gnu.org/archive/html/bug-gnubg/2003-06/msg00178.html)

**Importance-weighted / doubly robust off-policy evaluation** (not fetched this session)
- Doubly robust OPE for RL combines a learned model/value with per-decision importance weights. It is unbiased when the weights are correct and lower-variance when the model is good, but it requires behaviour-policy action probabilities π_b(a|s) > 0 wherever the target policy acts. — [Jiang & Li 2016, arXiv 1511.03722](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03722); [Thomas & Brunskill 2016 (MAGIC), arXiv 1604.00923](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.00923)

### Inferences
- **Precondition mapping for this project.** StS2 is a single agent against chance, and the greedy policy is deterministic. AIVAT's action-correction terms are therefore identically zero for the agent's own moves, so AIVAT reduces to MIVAT. All available reduction comes from chance-event corrections. This is the same structure as backgammon dice luck, so the 3x to 10x trial reductions quoted by Zare are the more relevant prior, rather than the 99.8% Leduc self-play figure, which depends on known randomised strategies.
- **IS/DR do not apply to greedy-vs-greedy checkpoint comparison.** The importance weights are 0 or undefined. They become usable only if evaluation logs come from a stochastic (e.g. softmax/ε) policy with logged probabilities, and even then the weights over roughly 1000+ decisions per run would explode. Per-decision DR with a strong value model is the only sane variant. Overall this is low priority.
- **Antithetic seeds.** Discrete shuffles, card rewards and enemy rolls have no natural "antithetic" mirror. The practical analogue is GNUBG-style stratification: stratify the seed panel by seed-level covariates such as act bosses, Neow options, map shape and act-1 elite set, so that every panel covers the strata evenly.

### Gaps
- No published application of AIVAT/MIVAT-style luck correction to a roguelike deckbuilder was found. Expected reduction for StS2 is therefore unknown and has to be measured empirically.
- Exact DIVAT paper authors and its reported reduction numbers were not verified (only the snippet was seen).

## 2. How to detect 1–2 point win-rate improvements with limited compute: power, paired tests, SPRT (Fishtest/OpenBench), aggregate-metric practice, and the winner's curse

### Takeaway
Paired seeds help only through the discordant fraction d. Detecting a 2-point gain at 80% power needs about 7.85·d/δ² seeds, roughly 2,000 to 5,000 for d between 10% and 25%, and 1 point needs 4x that. A Wald SPRT on discordant seeds saves about half the expected sample size versus a fixed-n test, as in Fishtest. Selecting the best of K checkpoints on the same panel inflates its estimate by roughly SE·E[max of K normals]. That calls for a fresh confirmation panel and pre-registered gates, which is what spire-mentor already does for StS2.

### Cited Findings
- Fishtest switched to new SPRT bounds "to increase the chance of catching Elo gainers", together with a new opening book and pentanomial statistics, "to be more resource-efficient" (Stockfish 11 release notes). — [Stockfish 11 blog](https://stockfishchess.org/blog/2020/stockfish-11/)
- The SPRT LLR is log[L(Elo = Elo1) / L(Elo = Elo0)]. The test runs until the LLR crosses one of two bounds computed from α and β. Example α = β = 0.05. Common bounds: Stockfish LTC gainers [0, 2] Elo, STC [0, 3], general [0.5, 2.5], non-regression [−1.75, 0.25]. "Bounds are generally looser for weaker engines and tighter for stronger engines". Fast-chess's pentanomial support "can make tests finish faster". — [chessprogramming.org SPRT](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Sequential_Probability_Ratio_Test)
- Fishtest FAQ: Elo estimates of single SPRT patches come with large error bars, and Elo estimates of *passing* patches are biased. This is selection bias from the stopping rule. — [Fishtest FAQ](https://github.com/official-stockfish/fishtest/wiki/Fishtest-faq) (via search snippet)
- Pentanomial model: game pairs on the same opening with colours reversed are scored as 5-category outcomes (LL, LD, DD, WD, WW), and variance is computed on pair scores. vdbergh attributes the gap between trinomial and pentanomial variance to opening-book bias. For small Elo differences, normalized_elo ≈ logistic_elo / sqrt(1 − draw_ratio). — [vdbergh/pentanomial](https://github-redirect.dependabot.com/vdbergh/pentanomial); [vdbergh/simul](https://github-redirect.dependabot.com/vdbergh/simul); [Reckless progress log](https://github.com/codedeliveryservice/Reckless/wiki/Progress-Tracking)
- Agarwal et al. (NeurIPS 2021 Outstanding Paper): in the few-runs regime, point estimates mislead. On Atari 100k, conclusions from point estimates "differed substantially" from a full statistical analysis. Recommendations: interval estimates, performance profiles, IQM, and the `rliable` library. — [arXiv 2108.13264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264)
- StS2 precedent: spire-mentor promotes champions only through a "pre-registered A10 gate, 21.7% → 28.1% on 1,000 paired seeds". The new champion was then re-measured on a *separate seed panel* (400 runs: 30.0%, 95% interval 25.7–34.7%), where the previous champion scored 26.75% on the same seeds. One earlier champion "missed the pooled significance bar" and was promoted on its A10 result alone. — [spire-mentor README](https://github.com/pep299/spire-mentor)
- Note that the second-panel gain (+3.25 pts) is about half the gate-panel gain (+6.4 pts), consistent with winner's-curse shrinkage. The source does not claim this; it is my reading of its numbers. — [spire-mentor README](https://github.com/pep299/spire-mentor)
- SpireBench (10 seeds per cohort) states that ten seeds are "too few to reliably order neighbouring models". — [arXiv 2610.12596](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12596v1)

### Inferences (derived numbers; assume p ≈ 0.6, two-sided α = 0.05, power 80% → (z_{.975}+z_{.8})² ≈ 7.85)
- **Current precision check.** SE = sqrt(0.24/1000) = 1.55 pts, so the 95% half-width is 3.0 pts, matching the stated ±3. An *unpaired* difference of two 1000-seed evaluations has SE 2.19 pts, a half-width of 4.3 pts.
- **Paired design.** Per-seed difference D ∈ {−1, 0, +1}, with Var(D) ≈ d (the discordant fraction) when δ is small. Required seeds n ≈ 7.85·d/δ²:

  | discordant d | δ = 2 pts | δ = 1 pt | 95% half-width at n = 1000 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 0.10 | 1,963 | 7,850 | 1.96 pts |
  | 0.15 | 2,944 | 11,775 | 2.40 pts |
  | 0.25 | 4,906 | 19,625 | 3.10 pts |
  | unpaired | 9,420 per arm | 37,680 per arm | 4.29 pts |

  So, with 1000 seeds, even the paired sign test reliably detects only gains of about 3 to 4 points unless d is small. The project should measure d from its existing paired logs.
- **SPRT on discordant seeds** (Wald, Bernoulli). Let q = P(new model wins | discordant). Test H0: q = 0.5 against H1: q = q1 = 0.5 + δ/(2d). LLR increment: +ln(2q1) when only the new model wins, +ln(2(1−q1)) when only the old one wins. Bounds are ln(β/(1−α)) and ln((1−β)/α), which is ±2.944 for α = β = 0.05. Worked example, d = 0.15, δ = 2 pts: q1 = 0.567, E1[increment] = KL(q1‖0.5) ≈ 0.0089, expected sample size ≈ 2.65/0.0089 ≈ 297 discordant seeds ≈ 1,980 seeds under H1 (and ≈ 1,970 under H0). The fixed-n test at the same α = β = 0.05 (one-sided) needs ≈ 10.8·d/δ² ≈ 4,060 seeds, so SPRT saves about 50%. Requirements: seeds evaluated in a random, pre-fixed order; no peeking with fixed-n tests; and the reported effect size of a passing run is biased upward (Fishtest FAQ). Re-estimate on a fresh panel.
- **Pentanomial analogue.** The project's "pair" is the same seed played by both checkpoints, and the 4 outcomes (WW, WL, LW, LL) collapse to the sign test. The pentanomial gain in chess comes from opening-pair correlation, which seeds already provide. The remaining lever is to make the two trajectories stay correlated *after* divergence (Section 3).
- **Winner's curse.** For K checkpoints of truly equal strength with independent noise of SE s, the selected best is inflated by about s·E[max Z_K]. E[max Z] ≈ 1.16 (K = 5), 1.54 (K = 10), 1.87 (K = 20), 2.25 (K = 50). At s = 1.55 pts and K = 10 that is about 2.4 pts, the size of the effects being chased. A shared seed panel makes the checkpoint estimates positively correlated, which reduces the inflation of *differences*. Fitting hyperparameters or curricula to the panel over many rounds still overfits the panel itself. Remedies: (i) a selection panel and a disjoint confirmation panel, with the confirmation number as the only one reported; (ii) pre-registered gates as in spire-mentor; (iii) periodic rotation of the selection panel; (iv) shrink selected estimates, e.g. report the confirmation estimate only.
- **Aggregation (Agarwal).** With a single training run per configuration, the seed-level CI does not cover training-run variance. A 2-point "checkpoint improvement" can be within-run noise across checkpoints. If compute allows, compare across ≥3 training seeds using IQM or a bootstrap over training runs.

### Gaps
- No public documentation of OpenBench's exact SPRT defaults was fetched.
- Fishtest's current live bounds and its α/β were not confirmed from the Fishtest server itself, only from chessprogramming.org and forum snippets.

## 3. How to apply these methods given a learned value head (r = 0.92 vs MC on boss states) and a seed-controllable simulator

### Takeaway
The most promising unbiased tool is a MIVAT-style chance correction computed in the simulator. At each RNG consumption point, subtract V(realised successor) − E_chance[V(successor)], with the expectation estimated by forking the RNG K times. In paired comparisons, seed-level covariates cancel, so what matters is reducing post-divergence luck, either through such corrections or by making RNG streams decouple less. The precise reduction must be measured. Backgammon practice suggests 3x to 10x in trials if V predicts outcomes well.

### Cited Findings
- AIVAT/MIVAT corrections are unbiased for any *fixed* value function, and variance reduction depends on value quality. — [ar5iv AIVAT](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1612.06915)
- DeepStack used its own value network as the control variate (−85% SD). — [arXiv 1701.01724](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.01724)
- spire-mentor's StS2 AI is "non-clairvoyant", modelling chance nodes in MCTS rather than exploiting known RNG. — [spire-mentor](https://github.com/pep299/spire-mentor)
- In sts_lightspeed-based work, MCTS rollouts use the true future draw order, so the teacher "effectively sees the future". Distilling it into a policy failed (BC accuracy capped around 0.44 to 0.59, floor ~12). — [Jialeiv/sts-rl-agent](https://github.com/Jialeiv/sts-rl-agent); sts_lightspeed lists "Tree Search (best result, knowing the state of the game's rng)". — [sts_lightspeed](https://github.com/gamerpuppy/sts_lightspeed)
- spire-agent (StS1) uses "frozen trajectories and paired combat regressions … on identical RNG worlds" and promotes changes only after benchmark gains without regression. — [AttemorySystem/spire-agent](https://github.com/AttemorySystem/spire-agent)

### Inferences (proposed recipe; not validated anywhere)
1. **Estimator.** For run i with outcome W_i ∈ {0, 1} and chance events t = 1..T_i in the simulator:
   Ŵ_i = W_i − Σ_t [ V(s_t^{obs}) − (1/K) Σ_{k=1..K} V(s_t^{(k)}) ],
   where s_t^{(k)} are successor states from re-drawing that RNG call with fresh independent randomness from the same pre-chance state. The correction has expectation 0 for any V fixed before evaluation, so E[Ŵ] = E[W]. Use the win-probability head, i.e. V on the same [0, 1] scale as W. Natural chance nodes: shuffles/draws, enemy move rolls, card-reward offers, relic/potion drops, event outcomes, shop stock, and ?-room resolution. Seed-level map/Neow/boss generation is shared across arms and can be left uncorrected.
2. **Information subtlety.** V is a function of the agent's observation. Put the chance node where the RNG is *consumed* (e.g. the shuffle), not where information is revealed (each draw). The expectation must use the true conditional distribution given the full hidden state. Forking the simulator RNG does this automatically. If V is evaluated on observations that reveal nothing new at that node, the correction is ≈0 and harmless.
3. **Which V.** Use one frozen V for both arms in a paired comparison, e.g. the older checkpoint's value head or a dedicated evaluator. If each arm uses its own V the estimator stays unbiased, but the corrections no longer share structure. V is calibrated for the policy that trained it, which affects variance only.
4. **Cost.** K value-head forward passes per chance node, roughly a few hundred chance nodes per run times K = 4 to 16, on batched GPU inference. This is cheap compared with rollouts and needs no extra game outcomes.
5. **Measure the gain before trusting it.** On existing 1000-seed logs, compute Var(Ŵ) / Var(W), and for paired comparisons Var(Ŵ_new − Ŵ_old) / Var(W_new − W_old). Equivalent-sample multiplier = 1/ratio.
6. **Re-coupling after divergence (CRN).** Divergence destroys CRN because one shared RNG stream gets consumed differently. If the simulator allows it, derive each RNG stream from (seed, subsystem, floor/act index) rather than one sequential stream, so that card rewards, enemy rolls and shuffles on floor f stay identical across arms even after different choices. This is the "synchronisation" principle in simulation CRN. It changes the game's RNG semantics relative to the real game, which leaves fairness unaffected (both arms see the same distribution) but means simulator results are not exact replays of the real game.
7. **Conditional Monte Carlo at the divergence point.** The two arms share the prefix up to the first differing decision s*. E[W_new − W_old | seed] equals the difference of the two policies' values from s*. Spending extra rollouts from s* (forked RNG) on discordant-prone seeds can be more efficient than adding new seeds. It is unbiased if s*'s hidden state is held fixed and future RNG is resampled correctly.
8. **Simple control variates on seed covariates** (e.g. V at the start of the run, or act bosses) help single-model estimates (variance × (1 − ρ²)) but cancel in paired differences.
9. **Diagnostic decomposition.** The same machinery splits each run into luck (chance corrections) and decision terms V(after decision) − V(before). This attributes losses to decision categories (card pick, path, shop, combat). Judging the policy by its own V is circular, because a greedy policy is argmax under its own Q. Decision terms are only informative under an external evaluator such as search or rollouts.

### Gaps
- Whether the project's simulator supports forking RNG mid-run, or per-subsystem RNG streams, is unknown from here.
- No empirical variance-reduction number for luck correction in any roguelike exists in the sources found.

## 4. Slay the Spire 1/2 AI efforts: techniques for combat vs drafting/pathing, and measured results

### Takeaway
Strong StS2 agents in 2026 pair a fast faithful simulator with non-clairvoyant MCTS for combat and an RL policy for run-level decisions. spire-mentor reports Defect A10 at 22.0% (8000 sims/decision, simulator, v0.107.1, 500 runs), and its best Ironclad A10 reaches 30%. LLM agents reach 35% (GPT-6 Astra, all characters, A10, 15 runs per character). Defect-specific LLM results are 33% at n = 15. The project's ~60% Defect A10 would far exceed every published number. That makes cross-checking fidelity and version differences (v0.111.0 vs v0.107.1) important before drawing comparisons.

Visual computer-use harness (Inspect ReAct), 15 single-shot attempts per character per game, fixed arbitrary seeds, wiki access and notes allowed, StS2 frozen at v0.107.1. StS2 A10 results: GPT-6 Astra 35%, GPT-6.1 Sol 29%, GPT-6 Sol 13%, Claude Opus 5.5 7%, Claude Fable 5.1 4%, Claude Sonnet 5.5 3%, GPT-6 Luna 0%. Astra by character: Regent 53%, Ironclad 40%, Silent 33%, Defect 33%, Necrobinder 13%. Caveats: 15 runs per character, overlapping CIs, the vision interface may understate ability, and the benchmark may drift from the live version. — [Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/publications/ai-systems-are-improving-rapidly-at-slay-the-spire-and-its-sequel)

Covers all Ironclad and colorless cards; out-of-combat parts were listed as unimplemented at capture time. No win rates in the README. — [sts_lightspeed](https://github.com/gamerpuppy/sts_lightspeed)
- **Jialeiv/sts-rl-agent** (single author, not peer-reviewed). A ~100k-param MLP candidate scorer (412-dim observation + 368-dim candidate), trained with REINFORCE, handles all non-combat decisions (map, card rewards, shops, campfires, events); combat is sts_lightspeed MCTS. 50 held-out seeds, A0 Ironclad, no CIs: heuristics + MCTS@50k reach floor 31.2 / 6% wins, learned + MCTS@50k floor 42.5 / 14%. A card-reward-only policy plateaued at about floor 35. Difficulty ladder (sim2000) by average floor: A0 38.5, A10 30.1, A20 25.7, with 0% wins at A20. Six negative results on distilling combat (BC, DAgger, REINFORCE, attention scorer, value-net 1-ply, PUCT), attributed to the clairvoyant teacher. — [Jialeiv/sts-rl-agent](https://github.com/Jialeiv/sts-rl-agent)
- **bottled_ai**: rule-based. Combat enumerates play orders via graph traversal plus its own simulation, scoring ~40 weighted values. Card picks, boss relics and upgrades use prioritized lists; events use explicit conditions. No hidden information. Best listed result: Watcher "52% winrate" (ascension and sample size not given on the README). — [bottled_ai](https://github.com/xaved88/bottled_ai). In SpireBench at Ironclad A20, BottledAI cleared Act 1 in 20% of runs and won 0 runs (mean floor 11.3). — [SpireBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12596v1)
- **spire-agent** (LLM-assisted hybrid). sts_lightspeed MCTS for combat; potions are withheld from search unless the no-potion search predicts excessive HP loss. Card picks come from a graph of expert winning-deck templates (Baalorlord's run archive), and an LLM ranks full routes after deterministic simulation gates against representative future encounters. Claims multiple A20H wins with Ironclad and Defect; in 12 recent Ironclad runs the average final floor was 38.6, 6 reached Act 3 and 2 reached Act 4. — [spire-agent](https://github.com/AttemorySystem/spire-agent)
- **SpireBench** (Lowe & Keemink, arXiv 2610.12596, Oct 2026). 20 LLMs, Ironclad A20, 10 held-out seeds. Best: Claude Fable 5.1 with 90% / 50% / 20% Act 1/2/3 clears and 0% Act 4; mean acts cleared 1.60 against 2.52–3.06 for streamers. — [arXiv 2610.12596](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12596v1)
- Academic/student work: Driedger (DiVA) used an ANN for reward choices and map paths, with garbled reported numbers, so it is unreliable. — [DiVA thesis](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1565751/FULLTEXT02). An Edinburgh UG4 project (2023) compared deck-building algorithms, including predicting a card's future performance. — [Edinburgh project](https://project-archive.inf.ed.ac.uk/ug4/20233951/ug4_proj.pdf). Neither was read in full.

### Inferences
- **Recurring pattern across projects.** Search (MCTS, or exhaustive play-order enumeration) handles combat, and learned or curated policies handle drafting and pathing. Jialeiv's +11 floors from learning *non-combat* decisions, and the floor-35 ceiling of card-reward-only learning, suggest that pathing, shops and events carry a lot of value.
- **Comparability.** The project's ~60% Defect A10 vs spire-mentor's 22% (Defect A10, 8k-sim MCTS, v0.107.1) is a large gap. Possible explanations: version changes between 0.107.1 and 0.111.0 (balance), simulator fidelity differences (r33hab's README warns about unread card errors), or real superiority. A cross-check would replay a sample of project seeds in the real game, or compare per-encounter win rates on matched states. Unverified either way.
- **Clairvoyance trap.** Any search teacher or value target built from rollouts that reuse the seed's true future RNG leaks information that the policy cannot observe (Jialeiv's failure analysis). If the project's "MC rollouts" for the r = 0.92 value-head check reuse the true RNG, the correlation may be inflated relative to the policy's real predictive task.

- No StS2 RL project other than spire-mentor publishes A10 win rates with sample sizes. No peer-reviewed StS2 RL paper was found.
- A paper titled "Conquering Slay the Spire" was not found under that name.
- Monster Train AI: no projects found.

## 5. Other deckbuilders and drafting games: how cards are valued and what was measured (Hearthstone, LoCM, MTG/17lands, Balatro, Monster Train)

### Takeaway
Drafting research falls into four families: (i) imitation of human picks from large logs (MTG: Draftsim and 17lands-scale data, DNNs beating Naive Bayes and heuristics, DraftFM at 51–60% top-1 agreement on unseen sets); (ii) RL where the reward is battle outcome under a fixed battle agent (LoCM); (iii) evolutionary search with simulator-evaluated decks (Hearthstone, LoCM); (iv) outcome prediction of whole decks (MTG 2026). Pick-imitation accuracy is the dominant metric. Win-rate-validated drafting results are rare.

### Cited Findings
- **MTG, Ward et al.** ("AI solutions for drafting in Magic: the Gathering", arXiv 2009.00655, 2020–21). Over 100,000 anonymized human drafts from Draftsim. Agents compared: simple heuristic, expert-tuned heuristic, Naive Bayes, and a deep NN. The DNN best emulates human picks. Accuracy also analysed across draft position. — [arXiv 2009.00655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.00655)
- **MTG, DraftFM** (Brian Ward, arXiv 2608.19568, Aug 2026). A 1.6M-parameter discrete-choice policy that scores pack cards conditioned on the drafted pool. Cards are represented by frozen 775-dimensional vectors built from the public card record and a text embedding, with no card IDs. Trained on 149M human picks from 29 sets. Top-1 agreement on 3 fully held-out sets: 50.8%, 60.4%, 56.7% (chance ~7% at the opening pick). Win-rate evaluation "deferred". — [arXiv 2608.19568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19568)
- **MTG, Rigaux & Kashima** (CoG 2026, arXiv 2607.04782). An encoder producing set-contextualized card embeddings predicts *drafted deck strength* (outcome), with "consistent improvement over linear baselines". Presented as the first learned benchmark for outcome prediction in MTG draft. — [arXiv 2607.04782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04782)
- **LoCM, Vieira, Tavares, Chaimowicz** (SBGames 2020). Deep RL for arena drafting with three approaches that differ in how much draft history they use. The learned draft strategies "outperform those of the best agents of the game" (abstract; numbers not extracted because the PDF could not be parsed). An earlier SBGames 2019 paper applied pure RL to both draft and battle. — [SBC entry](https://sol.sbc.org.br/index.php/sbgames_estendido/article/view/19743); [SBGames 2020 PDF](https://www.sbgames.org/proceedings2020/ComputacaoFull/209690.pdf); [SBGames 2019 PDF](https://www.sbgames.org/sbgames2019/files/papers/ComputacaoShort/198299.pdf)
- **LoCM, Kowalski & Miernik** (arXiv 2001.01326). Evolutionary arena drafting in LoCM. "Active-genes" variants learn faster and yield statistically better draft policies. — [arXiv 2001.01326](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.01326)
- A search snippet attributed to a 2021 CCG-AI survey states that the COG 2020 LoCM competition winner used static card weights computed by harmony search for drafting. Exact source document not verified. — search listing including [arXiv 2105.01115](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.01115)
- **Hearthstone, García-Sánchez et al.** (IEEE CIG 2016). A genetic algorithm builds decks, and fitness is simulated play against a representative set of human-made decks. "Quite effective decks" (preliminary). A later summary says win rates improved against most archetypes. The Hearthstone deck space was ~4.11×10^72 decks in Sept 2018. — [NJIT "Exploring the Hearthstone deck space"](https://digitalcommons.njit.edu/fac_pubs/8454); [Evolving the Hearthstone Meta, arXiv 1907.01623](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1907.01623)
- **Balatro**: a self-reported community RL agent (fruit-fly connectome model) at ~20% wins, White Stake, Red Deck, with a custom trainer that seeks exploitable seeds. Unverified. — [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/balatro-fan-claims-they-trained-google-fruit-fly-brain-simulation-to-beat-the-game-reinforcement-learning-currently-has-the-model-at-20-percent-success-rate?ref=thetechmonk.io). A GPT-6 Astra-driven Balatro bot used extra simulation tools and gave no proper win-rate estimate. — [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/ai-news/gpt-6-astra-balatro-poker-reddit-champion/)
- **StS card valuation**: spire-agent uses distance to expert winning-deck templates. — [spire-agent](https://github.com/AttemorySystem/spire-agent). A DEV Community author trained a logistic-regression card scorer on 334 of their own A20 runs (41% win rate). — [DEV post](https://dev.to/doronsinger/part-3-good-models-finish-last-2n6a). spire-agent's map gate simulates the current deck against representative future encounters, which is lookahead valuation. — [spire-agent](https://github.com/AttemorySystem/spire-agent)

### Inferences
- Three card-valuation styles appear: static ratings (harmony-search weights, tier lists), deck- or pool-conditioned scorers (DraftFM, Ward's DNN, the project's candidate-action Transformer), and lookahead simulation against future fights (spire-agent's gates, Hearthstone GA fitness). The project already has the second. The third, a cheap "simulate the deck after the pick against the act boss or upcoming elites" feature or auxiliary target, is the main untried family in the sources found. It would make sense as an input feature or an auxiliary value target, not as a replacement for RL.
- Text-embedding card representations (DraftFM) help generalise to new cards. That matters for StS2 because content changes between versions (0.107 → 0.111).

### Gaps
- No primary-source AI paper on *Hearthstone Arena* drafting specifically was found. arXiv 2009.00655 turned out to be MTG.
- 17lands metrics (e.g. games-in-hand win rate) were not fetched, so their definitions and biases are unsourced here.
- The CoG 2024 U. Alberta MTG drafting paper URL returned 404.
- Monster Train RL/AI: none found.
- UrzaGPT (arXiv 2508.08382, LLM LoRA for card selection) was seen as a title only.

## 6. Expert/benchmark evidence on where agents are systematically weak at high ascension (hypotheses for a Defect A10 RL agent)

### Takeaway
The best-documented failure modes come from LLM agents (SpireBench, StS1 A20): ignoring the act boss when drafting, too few elites, over-resting and HP over-conservatism, and poor self-forecasting. Community Defect guides stress "stabilize defense and cycling first, then Focus/orb scaling", plus boss plans such as Frost-plus-Focus walls. These are only hypotheses for an RL agent. Each can be checked from per-run logs.

### Cited Findings
- SpireBench (StS1 Ironclad A20): "no model builds its deck for the act boss the map shows". Models fight 0.9–1.4 elites in Act 1 against 2.0 in telemetry and 2.6 for streamers. Models rest at 57–75% of rest sites against 23% and 9%. The route explains "about a fifth of the gap" to streamers. The top four models hold a median 90% of max HP at the Act 1 boss against 53% for streamers, i.e. too conservative. Area-damage card uptake correlates with strength (ρ = 0.76). The paper argues that by Act 3 a marginal card dilutes the deck, so card-reward policy should change over a run. — [arXiv 2610.12596](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12596v1)
- Jialeiv: a card-reward-only learned policy plateaus around floor 35, while learning all non-combat decisions (including pathing, shops and events) adds ~11 floors. — [Jialeiv/sts-rl-agent](https://github.com/Jialeiv/sts-rl-agent)
- spire-mentor uses extra search in elite and boss fights: a move prior, and 16k sims in boss fights against 4k elsewhere. Boss fights are where the budget pays. — [spire-mentor](https://github.com/pep299/spire-mentor)
- Defect StS2 community guides (non-peer-reviewed): three build families, Orbs, Status and Claw. — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/slay-the-spire-2/defect). The Frost-tank plan stacks Frost channels and Focus until the boss runs out of damage before the player runs out of block. — [BrokenBuilds](https://brokenbuilds.gg/slay-the-spire-2/guides/slay-the-spire-2-defect-frost-tank-build-guide). "Stabilize defense and cycling first", after which scaling takes over. — [STS2.gg beginner guide](https://sts2.gg/guides/defect-beginner-guide). Orb and Focus mechanics are explained in the Untapped guide. — [Untapped orbs](https://sts2.untapped.gg/guides/the-defects-orbs-mechanic-explained)
- Epoch: Defect is mid-pack for the top LLM in StS2 (33%), and Necrobinder is hardest (13%). — [Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/publications/ai-systems-are-improving-rapidly-at-slay-the-spire-and-its-sequel)

### Inferences (hypotheses to test from logs; not established)
- **Long-horizon scaling under-valued.** PPO with discounting and a combat curriculum may prefer immediate damage/block cards over Focus and orb-slot scaling, whose payoff comes in Act 2–3 bosses. Test: compare pick rates of scaling cards vs win-conditional pick rates, and check the death-floor distribution (boss deaths vs hallway deaths).
- **Boss-specific preparation.** Check whether the observation includes the act boss identity. If it does, measure whether draft and path choices differ by boss; if they do not, that matches the LLM failure mode.
- **Elite and risk calibration.** Compare elites fought per act and the rest-vs-upgrade ratio against the human figures above.
- **Deck dilution and thinning.** Compare deck size at each act boss, skip rate by act, and removal purchases at shops (the project added removal-price observation in format 17), across wins vs losses.
- **Where to spend evaluation compute.** If losses concentrate in a few bosses, a stratified panel or a boss-state evaluation suite measures those improvements with far less noise than full runs, using the luck decomposition from Section 3.

- Human A10 Defect win rates for StS2 by character were not found. The 95% human line is all-character and approximate (Epoch).
