# Data-side techniques for a strong but plateaued RL agent: curricula, start-state selection, self-imitation, reanalysis, off-policy reuse, ensembles, distillation, late exploration

Scope note: these notes cover the data side, meaning what the agent trains on and how stored data and frozen checkpoints get reused. Architecture and optimiser changes are out of scope. Project readings for the Slay the Spire 2 (StS2) Defect A10 PPO agent are in each "Inferences" block. Source status: items marked "(fetched)" were read in this session (Oct 2026). Items marked "(recalled)" come from primary papers I know but did not re-open in this session. Treat their exact numbers as needing a check before they appear in a final report.

## 1. Curricula and start-state selection (PLR, ACCEL, PAIRED, learning progress, reverse curriculum, Go-Explore, backplay, restarts in AlphaZero-style training)

### Takeaway
The strongest and most recent evidence says to pick start states by learnability (success probability near 0.5) or by regret / value error. Picking by raw loss rate is weaker. One 2026 AlphaZero paper reports late-stage gains from an already well-trained model, where uniform archive restarts gave nothing. The project's current "weight by loss rate" corresponds to AlphaStar's f_hard, and the literature generally prefers a variance-shaped weight like p(1−p).

### Cited Findings
- **Sampling For Learnability (SFL), NeurIPS 2024.** The regret proxies used by PLR/ACCEL (positive value loss, MaxMC) "do not correlate with regret but with success rate". PLR and ACCEL buffers had average learnability of only about 0.01–0.02, with success rates of 0.84–0.97. SFL scores levels by p·(1−p), keeps the top K, and trains on a mix of fraction ρ from the buffer plus fresh random levels. Defaults: N=5000 sampled levels, K=1000, ρ=0.5, buffer refreshed every T=50 updates. SFL-selected levels averaged learnability of about 0.22, at roughly 50–60% success. SFL beat DR, PLR and ACCEL on CVaR (worst α% of levels) in JaxNav, multi-agent JaxNav, MiniGrid and XLand-MiniGrid. On hand-designed MiniGrid test sets, the methods were similar. Stated limitations: it only fits binary-outcome, deterministic levels, since p(1−p) is a Bernoulli variance. Filling the buffer needs extra rollouts (slower than PLR in XLand). The authors attribute the bad PVL/MaxMC behaviour to poor value estimates under partial observability. — [Rutherford et al., arXiv 2408.15099 (fetched)](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.15099v3)
- **Regret-Guided Search Control (RGSC), ICLR 2026.** A learned "regret network" picks states "where the agent's evaluation diverges most from the actual outcome". Candidates come from both self-play trajectories and MCTS nodes. They go into a prioritised regret buffer and are reused as start positions. Results averaged over 9x9 Go, 10x10 Othello and 11x11 Hex: +77 Elo over AlphaZero and +89 Elo over Go-Exploit. **Starting from a well-trained 9x9 Go model**, RGSC raised the win rate vs KataGo from 69.3% to 78.2%, while "both baselines show no improvement". — [Tsai et al., arXiv 2602.20809 (fetched abstract)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20809)
- **Go-Exploit, 2023.** AlphaZero normally starts self-play from the initial state and only samples moves early, which limits exploration of deep states. Go-Exploit samples start states uniformly from an archive of visited or search states. Duplicates are allowed, so frequent states get more weight. It is more sample-efficient than AlphaZero in Connect Four and 9x9 Go, and its search control beats KataGo's. — [Trudeau & Bowling, arXiv 2302.12359 (fetched summary)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12359). The RGSC authors criticise it for treating "all states equally, regardless of their learning potential". — [arXiv 2602.20809](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20809)
- **Prioritized Level Replay (PLR).** Levels are scored by mean positive GAE magnitude (or L1 value loss), with a staleness term and rank-based prioritisation. On Procgen it matched the previous SOTA test return. Combined with UCB-DrAC, it gave "over 76% improvement in test return relative to standard RL baselines" (recalled). — [Jiang et al., arXiv 2010.03934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.03934). Robust PLR (only training on replayed levels) gives minimax-regret guarantees at equilibrium (recalled). — [Jiang et al., arXiv 2110.02439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02439)
- **ACCEL and PAIRED.** ACCEL edits high-regret levels with small mutations and keeps them in a PLR buffer. It is reported to beat PAIRED and PLR on zero-shot transfer in mazes and BipedalWalker (recalled). — [Parker-Holder et al., arXiv 2203.01302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.01302). PAIRED trains an adversarial generator against a protagonist/antagonist regret (recalled). — [Dennis et al., arXiv 2012.02096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.02096). SFL found that in its new setting none of these beat plain domain randomisation. — [SFL (fetched)](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.15099v3)
- **Learning progress (ALP-GMM).** Task parameters are sampled where the absolute change in return over time is largest (recalled). — [Portelas et al., arXiv 1910.07224](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07224)
- **Reverse curriculum.** Start states are grown backwards from the goal, and training keeps only "good starts" with success in [R_min, R_max] = [0.1, 0.9] (recalled). This is the same intermediate-difficulty idea as SFL. — [Florensa et al., arXiv 1707.05300](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05300); GoalGAN uses the same band for goals — [Florensa et al., arXiv 1705.06366](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.06366)
- **Restarting from saved demo or archive states.** Salimans & Chen restarted episodes from progressively earlier states of one demonstration and reached 74,500 on Montezuma's Revenge (recalled). — [arXiv 1812.03381](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.03381). Backplay does the same in Pommerman (recalled). — [Resnick et al., arXiv 1807.06919](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.06919). Exploring Restart Distributions restarts from previously visited states (recalled). — [Tavakoli et al., arXiv 1811.11298](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11298). Jump-Start RL rolls in a guide policy for h steps, then lets the learner act, and shrinks h over time (recalled). — [Uchendu et al., arXiv 2204.02372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02372)
- **Go-Explore ("first return, then explore").** The method returns to promising archived states without exploring on the way there, then explores from them. The return step relies on restoring the simulator state. It handles stochasticity through a separate "robustification" phase: imitation via the backward algorithm, trained under sticky actions (recalled). — [Ecoffet et al., Nature 2021 / arXiv 2004.12919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12919)
- **AlphaStar PFSP opponent weighting.** f_hard(x) = (1−x)^p focuses on opponents the agent loses to. f_var(x) = x(1−x) focuses on opponents near 50%. The main agents mixed self-play with PFSP (recalled: about 35% self-play, 50% PFSP vs all past players, 15% vs forgotten players and exploiters). — [Vinyals et al., Nature 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1724-z)
- **DouZero+ "coach network".** This is start-state screening in a stochastic card game. A coach predicts whether an initial deal is balanced, and unbalanced deals are skipped. The acceptance threshold starts at 0 and rises during training. Together with opponent modelling, the agent ranked first on Botzone among more than 400 agents. — [Zhao et al., arXiv 2204.02558 (fetched summary)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02558). A 2024 follow-up says the DouZero+ variants do not give quantifiable improvements over DouZero. — [DouRN, arXiv 2403.14102](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.14102v1). This is conflicting evidence, so treat the size of the gain as unverified.
- **GT Sophy.** Training used hand-crafted "mixed scenarios" per track (starts at each grid position, specific situations, specialised opponents). "Multi-table experience replay" split the buffer by skill or scenario to keep data proportions fixed over training. 1-vs-N scenarios alone did not teach slipstream passing or starts. — [Sony AI blog (fetched summary)](https://ai.sony/blog/gran-turismo-sophy-five-years-on-from-nature-cover-to-open-frontier); [Wurman et al., Nature 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04357-7)

### Inferences
- **Change the boss-snapshot weight from loss rate to learnability or value error.** The engine can reseed hidden randomness without changing public state. That allows K reseeded rollouts per snapshot (e.g. K=8–16, greedy or sampled policy) to estimate each snapshot's p directly, which is exactly SFL's Bernoulli setting. Weight by p(1−p), or by mean |V(s0) − outcome| as an RGSC-style regret proxy. Do not use (1−p). Loss-rate weighting oversamples snapshots that are close to unwinnable, where the gradient is mostly noise. That is AlphaStar's f_hard, and AlphaStar kept f_var as an alternative for this reason.
- **The same reseed measurement is a diagnostic for the 61% of losses at the act-3 bosses.** If many pre-boss snapshots have p near 0, those losses are decided by deck and relics (out-of-combat play), and a combat-only curriculum will saturate. If p is spread around 0.3–0.7, combat play is the bottleneck and the boss curriculum still has headroom.
- **Snapshot staleness matters.** Snapshots from older checkpoints reflect older deck-building policies. GT Sophy's fixed per-table proportions and PLR's staleness term both argue for refreshing the archive and fixing the mixing fractions explicitly. Without that, the curriculum mix drifts as the archive grows.
- **Mid-run starts deserve a second look in a learnability-filtered form.** RGSC and Go-Exploit gain specifically from starting at deep states, and RGSC still helped when starting from a strong model. A small fraction of mid-run starts (pre-act-3 card rewards, shops, events) chosen by p(1−p) or value error is a different experiment from the earlier uniform mid-run starts. It is worth testing before ruling mid-run starts out.
- **Keep the value net's input distribution honest.** If curriculum episodes are scored by value error, the value net must be good on them. SFL's finding that PVL tracks success rate under partial observability is a warning that GAE-based PLR scores may not work here. Direct outcome sampling via reseed is more reliable.

### Gaps
- None of the curriculum papers above test a stochastic, long-horizon card roguelike. SFL explicitly assumes deterministic levels. Using reseeds gives a per-snapshot p over hidden randomness, which goes beyond SFL's stated assumptions. I found no paper that validates this.
- I could not retrieve RGSC's prioritisation formula or regret-network training details from the abstract page.
- I found no quantified ablation of the DouZero+ coach's contribution. A follow-up disputes the improvement.

## 2. Self-imitation, top-k / return-conditioned reinforcement, best-of-N filtering, AlphaZero-style reanalysis; risk of fitting to luck

### Takeaway
SIL and return-conditioning help in sparse-reward, mostly deterministic tasks. In stochastic environments they are biased toward trajectories that were lucky, and several papers name this failure directly. Reanalysis, meaning recomputing targets on stored data with the current network or search, is the variant best supported in strong agents. In this project, hidden-randomness reseeding allows expected-outcome targets in place of single-outcome ones.

### Cited Findings
- **SIL.** SIL imitates past actions only where the return R exceeds the current value, with loss on max(R − V, 0). A2C+SIL improved hard-exploration Atari (e.g. Montezuma's Revenge), and PPO+SIL improved MuJoCo (recalled; exact scores not re-checked). — [Oh et al., ICML 2018, arXiv 1806.05635](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.05635)
- **SIL can converge to sub-optimal policies** when the landscape has local optima, such as a second, smaller goal. The authors use a diverse ensemble of self-imitating policies to counter this. — [Gangwani et al., ICLR 2019, arXiv 1805.10309 (fetched summary)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.10309)
- **Self-imitation-style goal-conditioned methods (WGCSL, GoFar, DWSL) assume deterministic dynamics**, which "may lead to overly optimistic performance assessments in stochastic environments". Pure self-imitation is "highly sensitive to action noise". — [GCHR, arXiv 2508.06108 (fetched summary)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.06108)
- **Return-conditioned supervised learning (Decision Transformer, RvS) fails in stochastic environments.** Conditioning on achieved return confounds skill with luck. ESPER fixes this by clustering trajectories and conditioning on the expected return of the cluster (recalled). — [Paster et al., NeurIPS 2022, arXiv 2205.15967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15967)
- **AlphaStar's UPGO** is a self-imitation-like update in a strong agent. It bootstraps with the return when the next action did better than its value, and uses V otherwise. AlphaStar applied it alongside V-trace (recalled). — [Vinyals et al., Nature 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1724-z)
- **MuZero Reanalyse / MuZero Unplugged.** Stored trajectories are re-searched with the latest network to recompute policy and value targets. The "Reanalyse fraction" sets the share of targets from reanalysed data (0% = pure online, 100% = fully offline). One configuration set SOTA on RL Unplugged offline and on Atari 200M frames online. — [Schrittwieser et al., NeurIPS 2021, arXiv 2104.06294 (fetched summary)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06294); preliminary version in [MuZero, arXiv 1911.08265](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08265)
- **Suphx run-time policy adaptation (pMCPA)** is a reanalysis-like test-time method. Each round it simulates 100K trajectories under the hidden information still possible, then does a policy-gradient update. The adapted agent won 66% of head-to-head comparisons against the non-adapted agent. It was evaluated on only hundreds of rounds because of cost, and the gains were largest in the last one or two rounds. — [Li et al., arXiv 2003.13590 (fetched ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.13590)

### Inferences
- **Plain SIL or best-of-N "wins only" filtering is high-risk here.** A10 Defect outcomes mix skill with draw order and enemy RNG. max(R − V, 0) is positive on every lucky win, so SIL pushes toward the action sequences that happened to precede wins. The ESPER result predicts this. Under gamma=1 with a single terminal outcome, the bias reaches every action in a winning run.
- **A safer variant uses reseeding to replace the outcome with an expected outcome.** From a stored state (a snapshot at combat start, or any decision point the engine can restart from), run K reseeded rollouts. Use the mean outcome as a lower-variance target, or as the advantage baseline. This is reanalysis in the Monte-Carlo sense, and it matches pMCPA's approach of sampling hidden information. Because the engine cannot clone mid-combat states, this works at combat starts and run-level decision points but not within a combat.
- **A cheap form of reanalysis for PPO** is recomputing GAE with the current value net on recent data. PPG's auxiliary phase is one way to do this (see §3). Full MuZero-style reanalysis needs a model or search, and this project does not have one.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that quantifies the "lucky outcome" bias of SIL in a stochastic game, or that proposes a fix based on reseeding. GCHR and Paster et al. are the closest.
- I found no evidence that SIL or top-k filtering produced late-stage gains in any of the named game agents (Suphx, DouZero, OpenAI Five, GT Sophy). AlphaStar's UPGO is the closest analogue, and its separate contribution at the end of training is not quantified in what I read.

## 3. Off-policy and replay data mixed into on-policy training (V-trace/IMPALA, ACER, off-policy PPO, Reanalyze); sample efficiency in strong agents

### Takeaway
Off-policy corrections (V-trace, ACER, GePPO) mostly make it possible to scale up or to reuse slightly stale data safely. Evidence that replay mixing breaks a plateau in an already-strong agent is thin. The best-supported "reuse" in game agents is either distillation (PPG) or reanalysis with search.

### Cited Findings
- **IMPALA's V-trace** corrects for lag between actor and learner policies with truncated importance weights. It enabled large-scale distributed training on DMLab-30 and Atari (recalled). — [Espeholt et al., arXiv 1802.01561](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.01561). AlphaStar used V-trace + UPGO (recalled). — [Nature 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1724-z)
- **ACER** combines retrace targets, truncated importance sampling with bias correction, and replay (recalled). — [Wang et al., arXiv 1611.01224](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01224)
- **GePPO** extends PPO's improvement bound to data from several recent policies, with a generalised clipping rule and an adaptive learning rate. It was tested only on Gym/MuJoCo continuous control. Authors' limitations: the guarantees hold only approximately because of clipping, and the penalty constant may be too large to be practical. — [Queeney et al., NeurIPS 2021, arXiv 2111.00072 (fetched summary)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00072)
- **Phasic Policy Gradient (PPG)** alternates PPO phases with an auxiliary phase. The auxiliary phase trains the value function on stored data and distils it into the policy network under a KL constraint. It reported large sample-efficiency gains over PPO on Procgen (recalled). — [Cobbe et al., arXiv 2009.04416](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.04416)
- **MuZero Reanalyse** lets one configuration run at any data budget by changing the Reanalyse fraction. — [arXiv 2104.06294 (fetched summary)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06294)
- **OpenAI Five** used PPO on somewhat stale data at very large scale and kept training across code changes with "surgery". It played 80% of games against the current policy and 20% against past versions (recalled). — [Berner et al., arXiv 1912.06680](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06680)

### Inferences
- With billions of on-policy steps already available, sample efficiency is probably not the binding constraint. Extra replay of old full runs would mostly add stale, lower-skill data. If reuse is wanted, PPG-style value retraining on recent buffers, or a fixed replay table of curriculum episodes (GT Sophy's per-table approach), are the lower-risk choices.
- V-trace is useful when the snapshot archive or a frozen-expert pool produces data from other policies, for example when training on trajectories generated by archived checkpoints. Without a correction, those trajectories would bias PPO's ratio-clipped update.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled study showing that adding replay or off-policy correction to a converged, large-scale PPO game agent improves its final strength. GePPO's evidence is MuJoCo only, and I did not retrieve its numbers.

## 4. Ensembles and distillation (policy distillation, kickstarting, PBT, bigger students, test-time checkpoint ensembles, MoE / state routing)

### Takeaway
Distillation from several teachers is well supported and can beat the teachers. Kickstarting reached a from-scratch agent's performance in almost 10x fewer steps and finished 42% higher. Test-time aggregation by majority vote or Boltzmann multiplication is a solid, cheap baseline. However, apparent "complementarity" between checkpoints on fixed seeds can come mostly from chance in stochastic games. Test whether it is predictable before building a router.

### Cited Findings
- **Kickstarting** (distillation from teachers plus PBT, no architecture constraints, IMPALA on DMLab-30). A student learning from several specialist teachers matched a from-scratch agent's performance in "almost 10x fewer steps" and ended 42% higher than that agent's final performance. — [Schmitt et al., arXiv 1803.03835 (fetched summary)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.03835)
- **Policy distillation** can compress DQN policies into much smaller networks and merge several single-game policies into one multi-task network (recalled; exact compression and score figures not re-checked). — [Rusu et al., arXiv 1511.06295](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06295)
- **Distilling Policy Distillation.** Student-driven sampling (rollouts by the student, targets from the teacher, as in DAgger) has better theoretical and empirical properties than teacher-driven sampling (recalled). — [Czarnecki et al., arXiv 1902.02186](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.02186)
- **Reincarnating RL / QDagger** transfers an existing agent's policy into a new, possibly larger network, then keeps training with RL. The reincarnated agent beat training from scratch at a fraction of the compute (recalled). — [Agarwal et al., NeurIPS 2022, arXiv 2206.01626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01626)
- **Population Based Training** searches hyperparameters and copies weights during training. It was used in kickstarting and in FTW/AlphaStar-style leagues (recalled). — [Jaderberg et al., arXiv 1711.09846](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.09846)
- **Test-time aggregation.** Majority voting and Boltzmann multiplication are standard rules. Earlier studies found majority voting "a powerful aggregation scheme". — [ESANN 2013](https://www.esann.org/sites/default/files/proceedings/legacy/es2013-93.pdf); [Ensemble RL survey, arXiv 2303.02618](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.02618). In ensemble-based exploration, evaluating with a majority-vote policy over members gave significant gains in almost all Atari games. — [Lin et al., "The Curse of Diversity in Ensemble-Based Exploration", ICLR 2024, arXiv 2405.04342 (fetched summary)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04342)
- **Curse of diversity.** Individual members of an ensemble trained on shared diverse data do worse than single agents, because only a small share of the training data is self-generated. — [Lin et al., arXiv 2405.04342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04342)
- **Mixture-of-experts layers** (Soft MoE) let value-based deep RL networks scale with parameter count, where dense scaling did not (recalled). — [Obando-Ceron et al., ICML 2024, arXiv 2402.08609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08609)

### Inferences
- **Check whether complementarity is real before building a router.** If two checkpoints each win 60% of seeds independently, they disagree on 2 × 0.6 × 0.4 = 48% of seeds, and the oracle "best of the two" wins 1 − 0.4² = 84% of seeds by chance alone. A router can only capture complementarity it can predict from state. Test: take each fixed seed and reseed the hidden randomness while keeping the public start state. Then check whether "checkpoint A beats B on this seed" persists across reseeds. If it does not, the per-seed complementarity is noise, and only average-quality differences remain.
- **Evaluation resolution.** With 1000 seeds at p≈0.6, the binomial standard error is √(0.24/1000) ≈ 1.55 points, so the 95% CI is about ±3 points for one checkpoint. Paired comparisons on the same seeds cut this a lot. Gains of 1–2 points need paired tests or more seeds (variance-reduction ideas like AIVAT in poker, recalled — [Burch et al., arXiv 1612.06915](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.06915)).
- **Lowest-cost ensemble.** First try logit averaging (Boltzmann multiplication) or a majority vote over 3–5 archived checkpoints at every decision, on the 1000 seeds. If it beats the best single checkpoint by a significant paired margin, distil it back into one network: student-driven rollouts, KL to the ensemble, plus the PPO loss (kickstarting with a decaying weight). This keeps one deployable model and avoids building a router.
- **Distil into a bigger student.** Reincarnating RL supports this, but the plateau needs to be shown to be capacity-limited first, which is outside this report's scope.

### Gaps
- I found no published head-to-head comparison of checkpoint ensembles (majority vote, Boltzmann multiplication, state router) in a stochastic card or board game. The evidence is from Atari or MARL.
- I found no paper measuring how much of "different checkpoints win different seeds" is predictable versus chance in a stochastic environment. The calculation above is my own.

## 5. Exploration in late training (entropy schedules, count-based / RND, diversity populations)

### Takeaway
Novelty bonuses are built for sparse-reward, hard-exploration tasks. In environments full of hidden randomness they are distracted by noise, and there is little evidence that they help strong agents in stochastic games. Choosing which states to start from (§1) is a better-supported way to "explore" late in training than adding action noise.

### Cited Findings
- **RND** rewards prediction error against a fixed random network. It achieved strong Montezuma's Revenge results. Its deterministic target was designed partly to avoid the "noisy TV" problem of forward-model curiosity (recalled). — [Burda et al., arXiv 1810.12894](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.12894). NGU and Agent57 add episodic novelty and a meta-controller that picks exploration levels per actor (recalled). — [Badia et al., arXiv 2002.06038](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06038); [arXiv 2003.13350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.13350)
- **Entropy regularisation** smooths the optimisation landscape and helps escape some local optima, but its effect depends on the environment (recalled). — [Ahmed et al., ICML 2019, arXiv 1811.11214](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11214)
- **Diverse ensembles hurt their members** (curse of diversity, §4). — [arXiv 2405.04342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04342)
- **Go-Explore's thesis** is that exploring from returned-to states beats adding noise along the whole trajectory, because noise "derails" the return to frontier states (recalled). — [arXiv 2004.12919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12919)

### Inferences
- **The 9-point drop in training win rate from forced re-selection is expected on-policy cost.** It is not evidence for or against the exploration. Judge exploration only on greedy evaluation over the fixed seeds, with paired comparisons.
- **Out-of-combat exploration** (deck building) is where novelty bonuses would be most plausible. Even there, a cheaper analogue of "first return then explore" is to restart from learnability-filtered mid-run snapshots and explore only from those states.

### Gaps
- I found no evidence that RND, count-based bonuses or diversity populations improved an already-strong agent in a stochastic card game. None of the named systems (Suphx, DouZero, OpenAI Five, GT Sophy, AlphaStar) credit late gains to novelty bonuses in what I read.

## 6. Evidence from DouZero, Suphx, AlphaStar, OpenAI Five, GT Sophy, poker/bridge about late-stage data-side gains

### Takeaway
The data-side changes these systems credit are opponent and league composition (AlphaStar PFSP, OpenAI Five's past-self ratio), scenario or start-state design (GT Sophy, DouZero+ coach), privileged-information training (Suphx oracle guiding, PerfectDou), and test-time adaptation by sampling hidden information (Suphx pMCPA). None reports a clean late-stage ablation. The strongest "late gain from a trained model" evidence is RGSC in Go.

### Cited Findings
- **Suphx.** The order of gains was SL < RL-basic < RL-1 (+ global reward prediction) < RL-2 (+ oracle guiding). These were shown only as box plots, and the paper does not rank the components numerically. Each RL agent trained on 1.5M games over about 2 days on 44 GPUs. The online agent is RL-2 trained on about 2.5M games. Oracle guiding: an agent with perfect information is gradually turned into one that sees only observable information. pMCPA won 66% of head-to-head comparisons (§2). Suphx reached 10 dan on Tenhou. — [Li et al., arXiv 2003.13590 (fetched)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.13590)
- **PerfectDou** applies "perfect-training-imperfect-execution" (a privileged critic) to DouDizhu (recalled). — [Yang et al., NeurIPS 2022, arXiv 2203.16406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16406)
- **DouZero** combined Deep Monte-Carlo with action encoding and ranked first among 344 Botzone agents (recalled). — [Zha et al., ICML 2021, arXiv 2106.06135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06135). The DouZero+ coach filters starting deals (§1). Its gain is disputed. — [arXiv 2204.02558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02558); [arXiv 2403.14102](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.14102v1)
- **AlphaStar** used a league with main agents, main exploiters and league exploiters, PFSP weighting (f_hard vs f_var), V-trace + UPGO, and a KL term toward human statistics (recalled). — [Nature 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1724-z)
- **OpenAI Five** played 80% against the current policy and 20% against past versions, used surgery to keep training across environment and model changes, and trained for about 10 months. A "Rerun" from scratch with the final setup reached comparable strength with much less compute (recalled; exact Rerun figures not re-checked). — [Berner et al., arXiv 1912.06680](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06680)
- **GT Sophy** credits mixed-scenario training and multi-table replay (§1). — [Sony AI blog](https://ai.sony/blog/gran-turismo-sophy-five-years-on-from-nature-cover-to-open-frontier)
- **RGSC** improved a well-trained 9x9 Go model from 69.3% to 78.2% vs KataGo, where Go-Explore-style uniform restarts and vanilla AlphaZero did not improve. — [arXiv 2602.20809 (fetched)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20809)

### Inferences
- **Two ideas transfer most directly to StS2.** (a) Start-state screening by value (DouZero+ coach, RGSC), which maps onto the boss-snapshot curriculum with learnability or regret priority. (b) Training with privileged hidden information plus sampling the hidden information (Suphx oracle guiding and pMCPA), which maps onto a critic that sees the draw pile and RNG, and onto reseed-based expected-outcome targets. The memory note about a "known draw top" observation fits this direction.
- **League or opponent-composition ideas do not apply** to a single-agent PvE game. The analogous lever is the mix of enemy encounters, bosses and start snapshots, with explicit fixed proportions (GT Sophy).
